// README.md

How I build

I'm a builder — 15+ years of it in wireless telecom, from field drive testing to leading AI-first automation for one of the largest networks in the world. The domain taught me the discipline; the method transfers to any industry. Every system on this page began the same way: a problem nobody assigned me, stripped to first principles, automated, and shipped. Telecom-grown, industry-agnostic — the next problem doesn't need to have an antenna on it.

🧪

First principles

Strip a problem to what's actually true — the data, the physics, the user's pain — and build up from there, not from how it's always been done.

🕸️

Systems thinking

No fix in isolation: every tool considers the pipeline upstream, the engineer downstream, and the feedback loop that keeps it honest. Optimize the system, not the symptom.

🌱

Growth mindset

Field & RF → RF performance → data engineering → system architecture → solution architecture → automation → AI. Every stage self-taught before it was job-required.

⚙️

Automation

If a human does it twice, build the pipeline. The scoreboard is hours handed back to engineers: about 0.9 FTE a year so far.

💎

Value creation

The only metric that counts: hours returned to engineers, decisions made faster, problems that stay solved. Tools are a means; value is the product.

// POINT OF VIEW

Judgment in the age of AI

Three beliefs that decide what I build — and what I don't.

01 · SIGNAL
Content is cheap.
Judgment is scarce.

I started my career measuring signal-to-noise on drive tests. The tools changed; the job didn't.

  • A KPI that doesn't drive a decision is noise.
  • Baseline first. Anomaly second. Action third.
  • Fewer, louder alerts beat many quiet ones.
Proof: same-weekday baselines → ~1,860 hrs/yr returned
02 · HUMAN MANDATE
AI proposes.
Humans decide.
Humans stay accountable.

You can delegate the work to a model. You can't delegate the accountability.

  • Reversibility sets the level of autonomy.
  • Explain it, or don't act on it.
  • Keep the craft — you can't audit what you can't do.
  • Be open — say when AI did the work.
  • Be honest — report what failed, not just what shipped.
Proof: see the autonomy ladder ↓
03 · BUILD VS BUY
Buy the model.
Build the domain.
Open the interfaces.

Good builders know when not to build.

  • AI cut the cost to build ~83%. The cost to maintain didn't move.
  • Build only what someone will own in year three.
  • Default to open standards — and contribute back.
Proof: Claude (bought) + 75 skills (built) + MCP (open)
